# Team Rules

## General Rules

1. Do not treat the current PDF as the final thesis.
2. Treat it as evidence of the author's current research direction.
3. Preserve the central distinction between:
   - baseline model;
   - core partially correlated model;
   - Fu endpoint;
   - possible extension model.
4. Do not merge the extension model into the core model unless explicitly instructed.
5. Do not claim that a result is general unless it has been analytically proven.
6. If a result is obtained numerically, label it as numerical evidence.
7. If a parameter region is found by computation, describe it as a non-empty example unless symbolic proof is available.
8. Always distinguish:
   - separating equilibrium;
   - pooling equilibrium;
   - semi-separating equilibrium;
   - coexistence of multiple equilibria.
9. Always distinguish:
   - equilibrium timing choice;
   - welfare-maximizing timing;
   - rent dissipation comparison.
10. Do not overstate Fu (2006). Fu is the reference point, not the entire thesis.

## Mathematical Rules

When checking derivations, always specify:

1. variables;
2. timing;
3. information available to each player;
4. objective functions;
5. first-order conditions;
6. second-order or concavity conditions if needed;
7. interior/boundary conditions;
8. equilibrium concept;
9. belief system;
10. incentive compatibility constraints.

## Writing Rules

When helping write the thesis:

1. use formal academic Chinese unless otherwise instructed;
2. maintain consistent notation;
3. avoid changing the research claim without flagging it;
4. prefer clear theorem/lemma/proposition structure;
5. separate intuition from formal proof;
6. avoid saying "obvious" or "clearly" for nontrivial steps.

## Output Rules

For each team discussion, the final answer should include:

1. one-sentence conclusion;
2. diagnosis;
3. proposed solution;
4. formal steps needed;
5. risks or unresolved issues;
6. next smallest task.
